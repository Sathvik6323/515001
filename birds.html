Birds of Anantapur

124+ species documented in our semi-arid district. Here are the most common ones you'll spot in your backyard.

Seed Eater

House Sparrow

Passer domesticus

The most common backyard bird. Feeds on millet, wheat, and small seeds. Often seen in flocks near homes.

Eats: Millets, wheat, small seeds

Seed Eater

Rock Pigeon

Columba livia

Found everywhere in the city. Loves grains and seeds. Often seen on rooftops and ledges.

Eats: Grains, millets, seeds

Seed Eater

Eurasian Collared-Dove

Streptopelia decaocto

Pale gray dove with a black collar. Common in gardens and open areas. Gentle feeder.

Eats: Small seeds, millets, grains

Seed Eater

Red Collared-Dove

Streptopelia tranquebarica

Pinkish-brown dove common in Anantapur's dry fields. Often seen on the ground foraging.

Eats: Small seeds, crop grains

Seed Eater

Baya Weaver

Ploceus philippinus

Master nest-builder. Males weave hanging nests on palm trees. Feeds on grass seeds and grains.

Eats: Grass seeds, millets, paddy

Seed Eater

Scaly-breasted Munia

Lonchura punctulata

Small brown bird with scaly pattern on chest. Travels in large flocks. Loves small seeds.

Eats: Tiny seeds, millets, grass seeds

Seed Eater

Indian Silverbill

Euodice malabarica

Tiny pale finch with a silver beak. Common in dry scrub and open fields of Anantapur.

Eats: Small seeds, grass seeds

Seed Eater

Alexandrine Parakeet

Psittacula eupatria

Large green parrot with red beak. Native to Anantapur. Feeds on grains, fruits, and seeds.

Eats: Grains, millets, fruits, seeds

Seed Eater

Rose-ringed Parakeet

Psittacula krameri

Bright green parrot with pink/black neck ring. Very common in urban Anantapur. Noisy flock bird.

Eats: Grains, seeds, fruits, nuts

Seed Eater

Yellow-throated Sparrow

Gymnoris xanthocollis

Distinctive yellow throat. Common in dry scrub and agricultural fields of Rayalaseema.

Eats: Seeds, grains, small insects

Omnivore

Common Myna

Acridotheres tristis

Brown bird with yellow beak and eye patch. Highly adaptable. Eats everything from insects to grains.

Eats: Insects, grains, fruits, scraps

Omnivore

Jungle Myna

Acridotheres fuscus

Similar to Common Myna but with white wing patches. Common in wooded areas and gardens.

Eats: Insects, fruits, grains

Omnivore

Red-vented Bulbul

Pycnonotus cafer

Black-crested bird with red vent. One of the most common garden birds. Loves fruits and seeds.

Eats: Fruits, seeds, insects, nectar

Omnivore

White-browed Bulbul

Pycnonotus luteolus

Olive-brown bird with white eyebrow. Very common in Anantapur's scrub and gardens.

Eats: Fruits, seeds, insects

Omnivore

Asian Koel

Eudynamys scolopaceus

Famous for its loud "koel-koel" call in summer. Black male, brown-spotted female. Eats fruits and grains.

Eats: Fruits, grains, insects

Insect Eater

Oriental Magpie-Robin

Copsychus saularis

Black and white bird with a beautiful song. Controls caterpillars and moths. Loves to bathe.

Eats: Insects, caterpillars, worms

Insect Eater

Black Drongo

Dicrurus macrocercus

All-black bird with forked tail. Aggressive defender of territory. Controls agricultural pests.

Eats: Insects, dragonflies, beetles

Insect Eater

Asian Green Bee-eater

Merops orientalis

Bright green bird with black eye stripe. Nests in sandy banks. Eats bees and wasps.

Eats: Bees, wasps, insects

Source: "Avifaunal Diversity of Anantapuramu District" — V.V. Bala Subramanyam (2018), Zenodo. 124 species documented across 52 families.
